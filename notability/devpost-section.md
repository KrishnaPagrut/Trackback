## Project ideation & engineering notebook

We assembled a five-page engineering notebook around Trackback’s central question: **when one financial input changes, what else needs to change?** It connects the user problem to an exploration mind map, an end-to-end correction flow, the implemented architecture, and a timeline backed by Git commits.

The notebook makes our design tradeoffs visible: selective rework instead of blanket reruns, time-pinned evidence, deterministic calculations, and human review for consequential fixes. It also records problems encountered in the build, including stale database readers, replay inconsistencies and an interface that needed clearer explanations.

This is a retrospective synthesis of our code, decision log and tests—not a claim that every idea was documented before implementation. Stored evaluation results and current limitations are labeled explicitly.

Designed for review in **Notability**, the notebook includes editable vector diagrams, linked evidence and blank annotation areas. The review prompts invite us to challenge a tradeoff, trace a failure path, and assign the next engineering experiment. Authentic annotations can turn the notebook into a shared review record rather than a polished account of decisions left unquestioned.
