# Teleprompter: the demo film (silent, you read the subtitles)

Total 161 s (~2:40). Each scene's start time is when its first subtitle appears; read at an easy pace, the subtitles move at about three words a second and each one stays up a beat longer than it takes to say.

## 0:00 · Title

This is Trackback. In 2002, TrackBack pinged your page when someone built on it. We're reviving it for the numbers inside a company.

## 0:08 · Meet Tidewall

Meet Tidewall: six climbing gyms, forty million a year, and one promise to their bank: keep leverage under three times EBITDA. Finance is two AI agents, an analyst, Maya, and Dana, the controller.

## 0:21 · The source

April ninth: the billing system publishes its March export.

## 0:26 · Layer one

The Close Accountant closes the quarter on top of it: revenue by gym, EBITDA, trailing EBITDA of 5.6 million. Every piece of work records which rows it read.

## 0:36 · Layers two and three

The Covenant agent builds on those: leverage 2.95, the certificate for the bank, bonuses, tax. Maya builds the board deck on top, same tools, so her reads are logged too. That's the context graph.

## 0:49 · July 8: Trackback is switched on

July eighth. Tidewall switches on Trackback. First job: compare today's export with the version everyone read. $118,000, counted twice. So what else is wrong?

## 0:59 · The reconciliation

Trackback diffs the two versions, then follows the read receipts to the work that used those rows. It recomputes each number from its formula and checks it against Dana's materiality memo. Sentences get a fresh figure if the claim holds; otherwise, back to the author. Forty-four got a look; the rest were cleared, with reasons.

## 1:19 · One message per agent

Each affected agent gets one message, about only what it used; Maya gets the same, as a notice. “Your certificate rests on net leverage, now 3.03, over your covenant. Still valid?” It can't un-send a certificate, so it drafts a corrected one for the CFO.

## 1:37 · Jade, the reviewer

The agent called this immaterial, under the five percent rule. Jade, the reviewer, re-ran every call: over performance materiality, covenant breached, bonuses already paid. Overruled. Jade never writes a number; she checks them.

## 1:50 · The right person says no

Every fix goes to the person who can sign it: CFO, HR, controller. Dana says no to the first entry, dated into a locked quarter, so the agent re-drafts it in June. If it tries to approve itself, the database rejects the row.

## 2:06 · It learns

Both mistakes become proposed lessons. Before one goes live, it's re-run against every past case. The broad version fails: three old cases would flip. The rest pass, Dana approves, playbook v2. Held out: eighty to one hundred percent.

## 2:21 · Time travel

What did the agent know on April tenth? This. Ask for the July version from that date: refused, with both timestamps.
